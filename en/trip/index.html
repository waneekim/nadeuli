  • Joyang Bangjik, Ganghwa Island — A 1933 Textile-Mill Café with More to See Than Most Museums (Coffee and Bread a Bit Pricey)

    Our visit to Joyang Bangjik, a big café in downtown Ganghwa-eup near Ileokjo Sikdang. Said to stand on the site of a textile mill dating back to 1933, it's filled with so many old objects that there's more to see than in most museums — though coffee and bread are a bit pricey. A US Army school bus and a tow tractor, a wall of puffed-rice machines, a GoldStar TV and a mother-of-pearl table, pachinko machines and a wall phone, plus drink prices, the entry fee if you don't order a drink, hours and parking.

  • Mireuksaji Media Art in Iksan — The Night the Lost Wooden Pagoda Came Back in Light (Bring a Mat and Sit Up Front)

    Our Chuseok-holiday night at '2026 National Heritage Media Art Iksan' at the Mireuksa Temple Site: a wooden pagoda rebuilt in light between two stone pagodas, shows every 30 minutes from 7:30 pm, food trucks and chicken delivery on the lawn — plus tips on mats, seats, restrooms and parking for next time.

  • Earth Under Park, Iksan — A Café Overlooking a Quarry Through Glass Walls, Where Excavators Still Dig Stone Below (Chuseok Holiday)

    Our Chuseok-holiday stop at Earth Under Park in Hwangdeung, Iksan: a café where you look down through glass walls into a huge granite quarry cut down from a whole mountain — and excavators are still quarrying stone at the bottom. It was packed. Coffee, ice cream and baked goods; prices from the menu board, hours and parking from Naver Place, and the quarry's history from a Munhwa Ilbo article.
