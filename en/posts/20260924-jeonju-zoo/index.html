Jeonju Zoo with Kids on Chuseok — 3,000 Won for Adults, Bears, Tigers and Meerkats, Then Dreamland to Burn Off Energy

Our day at Jeonju Zoo with the kids 🐯

For Chuseok (the Korean harvest holiday)
our family got together,
and we all went
to Jeonju Zoo.

A tiger lying in the shade of a rock

📍 68 Sori-ro, Deokjin-gu, Jeonju
⏰ 09:00–19:00
(Nov–Feb: until 18:00)
Open every day of the year
💰 Adults 3,000 won
Youth and soldiers 2,000 won
Children 1,000 won
🅿 Parking available
📞 063-281-6759

🐾 Every enclosure full of animals

There were so many species
that nearly every enclosure
was full of animals.

The kids were so excited
they went to see every single animal.

I got swept up in it too,
and we went all the way round —
elephants, snakes, bears, birds and monkeys.

🐻 A brown bear and a blue drum

In the bear enclosure
a blue drum
hung from a chain.

It looked like it was put there
for the bear to roll and play with.

A brown bear lying by the water, with the blue drum

🦦 The longest stop: the meerkats

This is where the kids
stayed glued the longest.

One meerkat stood up on a rock
keeping a lookout,
while another one
scurried back and forth behind it.

A meerkat standing up on a rock (GIF)

🦜 A white parrot with a yellow crest

In the bird enclosure
this one caught our eye.

It sat on a wooden post
holding its food with its foot.

A white parrot with its yellow crest raised (GIF)

📖 A sign for every species

Every enclosure has a sign,
which made it easy
for the kids to read about the animals.

Here’s what the mouflon sign says,
as an example.

The mouflon sign

Mouflon (European mouflon)
Order Artiodactyla, family Bovidae · eats grass and leaves

It’s said to be the smallest
of the wild sheep.
Males have large horns;
females’ horns are very small.

It lives in the Middle East and West Asia,
and its IUCN Red List status
is Vulnerable (VU).

🌳 Laid out like a park

This is what the paths look like.

A wire-mesh walkway runs overhead,
and below it
a path lined with wooden fences.

A visitor path with a wire-mesh walkway running overhead

Overall, the route is neat and easy,
and the grounds are landscaped like a park.

And there are restrooms all over,
so getting around with kids was easy.

When you have kids in tow,
this makes a bigger difference
than you’d think.

🎡 Dreamland to finish

Inside the zoo
there’s a small amusement park
called Dreamland.

It’s just the right size
for younger elementary kids,
perfect for using up
all the noisy energy
left after the animals.

It reopened after a makeover
in February 2024,
and rides are paid for separately from zoo admission.

🔑 KICKAfter the animals,
finish at Dreamland inside the zoo!
(rides cost extra)

Dreamland’s rides and how to buy tickets → Read it here

🎫 Admission and hours

Adults (ages 19–65) 3,000 won
Youth (ages 13–19) 2,000 won
Children (ages 5–13) 1,000 won
Soldiers 2,000 won
(discounts for groups of 20 or more)

The zoo is run by Jeonju City,
so it’s really cheap.

Two adults and two kids
come to 8,000 won.

Mar–Oct 09:00–19:00
Nov–Feb 09:00–18:00
Open all year round.

Kids aged 4 and under
are charged the child fare
without proof of their date of birth.

(per Jeonju City’s official tourism guide)

👍 Who this is for

✔ Families together for a holiday
looking for somewhere to go
✔ Anyone looking for easy paths
and plenty of restrooms with kids
✔ Anyone who wants animals, then rides
to tire the kids out

So many animals that the loop is worth it,
cheap tickets, plenty of restrooms,
and rides at the end!

With the whole family together for Chuseok,
it was a great place to go.
