We’re splitting downtown Charleston

a quiet, peaceful waterfront.

🐴 Old Town, Where Horse-Drawn Carriages Go By

Downtown Charleston is known
for its Old Town.

On the street,
horse-drawn carriages were carrying tourists
around the neighborhood.

A tourist carriage passing in front of a brick building (faces of passengers blurred with mosaic)

The horse pulling the carriage — a shop with a red awning behind it

We heard this neighborhood used to be
a wealthy town that made a lot of money
from the slave trade.

Charleston was a major port
where people taken from Africa
were brought into America.
Apparently, some researchers estimate that 40% of the enslaved people
who came to America
arrived at one Charleston wharf (Gadsden’s Wharf).

Two big motorcycles parked along a palm-lined street

🌊 A Quiet, Peaceful Waterfront

Head toward the waterfront
and you reach Waterfront Park.
We heard it opened in 1990
along the Cooper River.

The waterfront was quiet and peaceful.

Marsh grass and wide water beside the wooden promenade

The marsh and park trees seen over the railing

KICKIt's a waterfront,
but there was no sea smell at all,
so it was nice to walk! 👃

Walk to the end of the pier stretching over the water
and the sea opens up wide.
A WATER TAXI went by
and seagulls were flying around.

The sea from the end of the pier — a passing WATER TAXI and seagulls

The view toward the harbor from the pier

A covered pier — tables and benches that are good for a rest

🍍 Charleston’s Landmark Pineapple Fountain

In the middle of the park
is Charleston’s famous pineapple fountain.

Water flows down
tier by tier from the big pineapple-shaped fountain.
Lots of people were taking photos.

The pineapple fountain from the side (faces of people nearby blurred with mosaic)
