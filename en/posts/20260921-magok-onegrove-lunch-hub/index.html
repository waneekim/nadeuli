Where to Eat at Magok One Grove — 4 Restaurants, Picked by Who You’re With

One Grove is a mall-and-office complex in Magok, western Seoul. From November 2025 to April 2026 we ate at four restaurants there — some days with the whole family, some days just the two of us. So this post isn’t a ranking; it’s a lunch map sorted by who you’re eating with.

Quick facts

  • 📍 One Grove, 165 Gonghang-daero, Gangseo-gu, Seoul (공항대로 165)
  • 🚇 Magok Station (Line 5), exit 1 — about 3 minutes on foot. From Gimpo Airport it’s 2 stops on Line 5 (Gimpo Int’l Airport → Songjeong → Magok)
  • ⏰ All four restaurants: 10:30–22:00 daily (last orders 21:00; Matsunohana 20:30)
  • 🅿 One Grove underground parking: first 30 min free, more free time if you spend more (details below)

🍜 With the whole family — Eulji Kkankkan (Vietnamese)

In November we ate here as a family. The pho broth was rich but clean, and the thinly sliced beef was tender enough that our kids ate it happily. The bean sprouts and coriander were fresh and fragrant.

Generous portions and friendly service make it an easy family pick. One thing to know: the seasoning is on the strong side, so adjust to taste.

  • Beef pho 13,000 won · B1 · last order 21:00

🍤 For a date — Matsunohana (Japanese rice bowls)

In February my wife and I stopped here on a date. We had the shoyu-salmon shrimp tempura bowl (ebi-tendon) and the shoyu-salmon hormone bowl — both impressive from the moment they arrived.

We added a side of wild conger eel tempura, which was wonderfully crisp. The savory hormone and the soft, umami-rich soy salmon went well together, and everything was neatly plated — it felt like being treated.

  • Shoyu-salmon ebi-tendon 18,800 won · B1 · last order 20:30 (the earliest of the four)

🍣 Eating alone — Hoho Sikdang (Japanese home cooking)

In April I ordered the salmon rice bowl and a braised dish. Everything was neat and tasty: the salmon was thick and fresh, and the wasabi-and-soy combination was clean.

The braised dish had a deep sauce that went perfectly with rice.

Side dishes and soup come with it, so it’s a filling Japanese home-style meal. In my Naver review I ticked “good for eating alone” and “good for conversation”.

  • Sake-don (salmon rice bowl) 18,000 won · Building D 1F · last order 21:00

🍛 With two kids — Bonghyeja’s Kitchen

In November the four of us — my wife, our two sons and I — came here. The pork cutlet curry beat our expectations: crispy outside, juicy inside, with a rich curry sauce and a generous portion.

The spicy stir-fry was well seasoned, with plenty of glass noodles and vegetables, but it’s a strong, adult-level heat — kids will need it toned down.

Side dishes and soup were fine, the table setting was tidy, and it was good value. A spice-level option would make it easier for families to order again.

  • Stir-fry dishes 13,000–15,000 won per person · Building A 1F · last order 21:00

🔑 The short version

  • Family → Eulji Kkankkan or Bonghyeja’s Kitchen
  • Date → Matsunohana
  • Alone → Hoho Sikdang

For visitors from abroad

  • Getting there from Gimpo Airport: Line 5 toward the city, 2 stops to Magok, exit 1. Taxi drivers will know “원그로브” (One Grove) or the address 공항대로 165 — copy the Korean under the map.
  • Maps: Naver Map gives the most accurate walking and transit directions in Korea; the Google Maps button under the map opens the same spot.
  • What we didn’t check: English menus, ordering method and foreign-card payment. If you’ve been, we’d love to know.
