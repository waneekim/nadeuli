Everland by Day and by Night — a Tulip Afternoon and a Summer Night

We went to Everland, the theme park in Yongin, Gyeonggi-do, twice in 2026: once on a Sunday afternoon in April and once on a Saturday night in July. The photos from the two days look like two different parks, so instead of a ride-by-ride review, this post lines the two days up in the order our photos were taken. We only describe what the photos actually show.

Quick facts

  • 📍 199 Everland-ro, Yongin (에버랜드로 199) — copy the Korean address below the map into a taxi or map app
  • 🚇 Public transport: Bundang Line to Giheung → transfer to the Everline light rail → ride to the last stop → free shuttle bus to Everland (Everland official guide)
  • 🚌 Buses from Seoul listed by Everland: 5002, 5700, 1500-2, 1113 (from Gangnam, Jamsil, Sadang and other stops)
  • ⏰ Opening hours change by date and season — check Everland’s official calendar before you go
  • 📞 +82-31-320-5000

April 19 (Sunday), 4:24 pm — the tulip street

We only kept four photos from April, and the first one sums up the day. A row of European-style buildings with steep gabled roofs, and in front of them a bed of tulips mixed with yellow flowers. A roller coaster track rises behind on the left, and the sky was completely clear.

A sign by the flower beds read “Tulip Festival — Big-scale Tulip Field, DIVE into the tulips”. According to Everland’s official festival page, the 2026 Tulip Festival ran from March 20 to April 30, so our visit on April 19 was about ten days before the end. The beds were still dense with color.

The next photo is almost two hours later, at 6:13 pm: one of our kids walking with a blue cotton candy, parasol tables under fresh green trees, and a cable car gliding by in the distance. The long shadows show the sun was going down. That’s where our April record ends.

July 25 (Saturday), 8:28 pm — a dome lit up

From July we only have night photos. Everland’s official blog said the park stayed open until 11 pm on Fridays and Saturdays from July 24 to mid-August, so July 25 was the first weekend of the summer night opening. A firefly festival (“Midsummer Night’s Firefly Festival”, July 24–August 30) was running at the same time.

The first shot shows a building with a domed roof and a thin spire lit in pink, with the moon above it and people strolling across the square.

Ten seconds later, the same building was white. The lighting keeps cycling through colors. At the right edge of this shot you can see a large white figure — the next photo explains what it is.

At 8:29 pm: a huge sheep sculpture sitting in the middle of the square — white body, dark grey face and feet, pinkish ears, and a gold bead necklace with a round pendant. A wooden sign at its feet said “귀여운 녀석들” (“Cute Guys”). Behind it you can see two panda sculptures, a sign reading “BAO HAUS”, a clock tower and the outline of a roller coaster.

According to Everland’s official blog (May 7, 2026), this is the black-faced sheep art sculpture, about 7 m tall, installed in the Global Fair area by the main gate on May 1. We couldn’t confirm on the official pages whether “Cute Guys” is the official name of the display, so we only report what the sign said.

9:20 pm — the carousel

The next batch jumps about fifty minutes to 9:20–9:22 pm at the carousel. Strings of light bulbs line the arches of the roof, a golden carriage and white horses go round, our kids split between a horse and the carriage, and soap bubbles float between them. People were lined up outside the fence.

We couldn’t confirm the carousel’s official name or zone on Everland’s pages, so we leave it out.

9:46 pm — the pandas and the sheep from behind

At 9:46 pm we were back in the square by the main gate. This time we photographed the two panda sculptures from behind — sitting with a few green boxes between them, fenced off with white posts, with the clock tower and the dome in the background.

Thirty seconds later, the sheep from behind: from the back it’s just a giant white mound, with the bead necklace running around the back of its neck. One of our kids walking toward it gives you a sense of the scale.

At 9:47 pm, looking up at its face from the front. At 8:29 pm the pink lighting made the whole sheep look pink; under white light you see its real colors — white wool, black face, pinkish ears, gold necklace. People kept taking turns for photos under it; we only use shots without faces here.

9:48 pm — the last shot

Our last July photo, at 9:48 pm, is a disc-shaped bench with a green alien figure holding a yellow star in a niche. The kids sat on it for a photo, so it isn’t in this post. The night opening ran until 11 pm, but our photos stop here.

Day vs. night, going only by our photos

  • April afternoon: flower beds, building colors and long shadows — not a single sculpture or light show.
  • July night: the opposite — lit buildings, sculptures and the moon. The same building changes from pink to white within ten seconds, and the 7 m sheep turns pink or white with the lights.
  • Our four April photos were taken at just two moments (4:24 pm and 6:13 pm), while thirty-three July photos fall within 1 hour 20 minutes (8:28–9:48 pm). We clearly took far more pictures at night.

Tips for visitors

  • The tulip festival (spring) and the summer night opening are seasonal — the 2026 dates above are over. Check Everland’s official site for the current year’s dates before planning.
  • If you go by public transport, the Everline light rail plus the free shuttle from the last stop is the route Everland itself recommends; Seoul buses are listed on the same official page.
  • Naver Map works well in Korea for transit directions; the Google Maps button under the map opens the same location.
