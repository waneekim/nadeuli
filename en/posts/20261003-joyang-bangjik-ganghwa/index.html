Joyang Bangjik, Ganghwa Island — A 1933 Textile-Mill Café with More to See Than Most Museums (Coffee and Bread a Bit Pricey)

Our Ganghwa Island big-café review ☕

After a jeotguk galbi lunch
(a soup with small ribs)
at Ileokjo Sikdang (일억조 식당),
we stopped at a café nearby,
Joyang Bangjik (조양방직).
Both are in downtown Ganghwa-eup.

(The name means
’Joyang Textiles’.)

The 'only 조양방직 Since 1933' sign in front of a rusted steel-plate wall

📍 Ganghwa-eup, Ganghwa-gun
12 Hyangnamu-gil 5beon-gil
⏰ Weekdays 11:00–20:00
Weekends & public holidays
11:00–21:00

In a word,
it’s a café packed with old things.

There’s more on display
than in most museums.

The catch: coffee and bread
are a bit on the pricey side.

🧱 Starting with the rusted steel wall

Even from the street,
the place feels different.

On a rusted steel-plate wall,
big white letters spell out
’신문리미술관’ (Sinmunri Art Museum), ‘Joyang Bangjik’
and ‘Since 1933’.

The steel wall: '신문리미술관 · 조양방직 · Since 1933'

A yellow standing sign by the wall
shows the opening hours.

Weekdays 11 am to 8 pm,
weekends & public holidays 11 am to 9 pm

Yellow standing sign — weekdays am 11–pm 8, weekends & public holidays am 11–pm 9

Grapevines cover the entrance
like a roof.

The banner beside it says
every Wednesday is ‘Salt Bread Day’,
with 30% off salt bread.
(A pillar hid the fine print at the end,
so we couldn’t read all of it.)

The entrance under the grapevines

🎫 Good to know first

On a wall inside, a sign:
‘소금빵 성지 조양방직’ — Joyang Bangjik,
the holy land of salt bread.

The notice below it says
the whole place is a ‘Care Kids Zone’:
if you come with children,
they ask you to actively look after them.

The salt-bread 'holy land' sign and the Care Kids Zone notice

And this notice
is one you really need to know.

If you don't order a drink, entry is 6,000 won for middle-school age and up

🔑 KICKIf you don't order a drink
entry is 6,000 won
for middle-school age and up!
Even if you only plan to look around
budget for a drink

🚌 Vehicles in the yard

The yard has some big vehicles.

A rusted green tow tractor
with ‘TP 55’ on its side
has its door open,
so you can peek into the driver’s seat.

Rusted green tow tractor 'TP 55'

There’s also a Blue Bird school bus
with its sky-blue paint peeling off.

The front plate says ‘US ARMY’,
and above it: ‘승하차시 추월금지’
(do not pass when loading or unloading).

You can even go inside the bus!

Blue Bird school bus — the US ARMY plate and 'do not pass when loading or unloading'

On one wall of the yard,
nine rusted puffed-rice machines
hang in a row on rebar shelves.

The note above them is fun:
‘Puff up your reverence
for all living things.’

Nine puffed-rice machines and the 'puff up your reverence' note

🏚 Step inside

Under a high wooden ceiling
it’s a wide, factory-like space.

Everywhere you look, there are old things.

In one corner, an old-fashioned room.

Brassware on a mother-of-pearl table,
a GoldStar (금성) television beside it
(GoldStar is LG’s old brand name),
a horse painting and an oil lamp behind,
a brass sprayer, even a ‘Salt’ (소금) sign!

An old-style room with a mother-of-pearl table and a GoldStar TV

The GoldStar TV — with a dial for changing channels

By the window: glass floats wrapped in netting,
a wooden cart, and
an old ‘BIRMINGHAM’ scale.

The scale has a note on it:
‘It breaks if you jump on it.’

Glass floats, a wooden cart and the BIRMINGHAM scale

On a workbench: lit-up
oil-can lamps and pachinko machines,
with a wagon wheel on the wall.

Oil-can lamps and pachinko machines, a wagon wheel on the wall

There’s also a wooden wall phone
with two bells and a dial.

Beyond all this — a motorbike, a marlin model,
cart wheels — there’s more than you can count.
Our photos only show part of it.

A wooden wall phone with two bells and a dial

(Per the Naver Place menu;
we couldn’t confirm bread prices)

🚗 Parking

There’s a dedicated parking lot.

Naver Place said
that when it’s full, you should use
the public lots nearby
(600 won per 30 minutes).
(Checked October 3)

👍 Who this is for

✔ Anyone looking for a café
with lots to see in Ganghwa-eup
✔ Anyone who loves looking at old things
(GoldStar TV, school bus, puffed-rice machines)
✔ Anyone looking for a stop after
jeotguk galbi at Ileokjo (also in Ganghwa-eup)

Coffee and bread are a bit pricey,
but you get the fun of browsing
more old things
than most museums have.

If you’re going with kids,
remember it’s a Care Kids Zone!
