Charleston, Part 3: White Point Garden with Its Row of Cannons and the Atmospheric St. Philip’s Church (US Business Trip, Day 7)

Part 3 of our Charleston downtown series is
the story of a seaside park
with cannons and cannonballs ⚓

It feels like
a war memorial park,
full of cannons and monuments,
and it is called White Point Garden.

Cannons lined up under huge oak trees

📍 White Point Garden
(southern tip of the Charleston peninsula, South Carolina, USA)
⚓ A park full of cannons, cannonballs and monuments
🌊 Next door: The Battery, a seawall promenade
😌 We rested here, looking at the sea
⛪ St. Philip's Church on the way back to town

⚓ A Park Full of Cannons and Cannonballs

It is a park of about 5.7 acres (about 23,000 m²)
at the very tip of the Charleston peninsula,
on the sea side.

Cannons and monuments
are placed all around the park.

A small cannon on wheels, on a brick floor

A cannon seen from the front, with the oak park stretching behind it

During the Civil War, a battery was placed on this spot,
and I heard that some of the cannons now here
were moved from Fort Sumter.

🚩 A Monument to Those Who Defended Fort Moultrie

The most eye-catching statue monument
honors the soldiers who held off the British fleet
at the Battle of Sullivan’s Island
on June 28, 1776.

The figure holding the flag at the top is
Sergeant William Jasper.
I heard he picked up the flag
and raised it again
after a cannonball knocked down the flagstaff.

Monument to the defenders of Fort Moultrie: a statue of Sergeant Jasper holding the flag, with a black cannonball in front

I heard this monument was put up in 1877.
There was also a black cannonball
placed in front of it.

🌊 Resting by the Sea

Right next to the park is
The Battery,
a seawall promenade along the shore.

I heard this is where the Ashley and Cooper rivers meet
to become Charleston Harbor.

The sea seen from the seawall promenade (faces of people blurred with mosaic)

Here we rested for a good while,
looking at the sea.

🚢 Torpedo Boat Crew Memorial

At a corner along the park path,
there is a stone marker.

It honors the Confederate crews
who served on torpedo boats
defending Charleston Harbor in 1863–1865.

It said that more than thirty of them
drowned, and that the names of only
sixteen are known.

Torpedo boat crew memorial: Charleston Harbor, 1863–1865

I heard this granite marker, put up in 1899,
honors the first successful submarine
(the H.L. Hunley).

On the side of the marker is a fish-shaped metal ornament,
and water trickles out of its mouth.

The fish-shaped metal ornament on the side of the marker, with water trickling out

⛪ On the Way Back to Town, St. Philip’s Church

After resting by the sea,
on the walk back to town,
a tall steeple suddenly appeared at the end of the alley!

It was atmospheric and beautiful.

A church steeple rising at the end of the alley

Its name is St. Philip’s Church.

I heard it is a church that began in 1680,
the oldest European-American church
community in South Carolina.

I heard the current building was built in 1836,
and the steeple was completed in 1850.

St. Philip's Church: a tall steeple with a clock

The church portico held up by thick columns

KICKI heard this steeple was once
a lighthouse guiding ships
into the harbor! (1893–1915)

👍 Who I’d Recommend It To

✔ Anyone curious about
Charleston’s history
✔ Anyone who wants to rest
while looking at the sea
✔ Anyone who likes old churches
and alley scenery

A walk where we rested by the sea
and even saw an atmospheric church!
