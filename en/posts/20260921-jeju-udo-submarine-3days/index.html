Udo Submarine in Jeju — What We Booked, Where It Leaves From, and Dinner in Seongsan

From May 2 to 4, 2026 our family spent three days in Jeju. This is more of a record than a review: all we kept were the Udo Submarine booking confirmation, the timestamps on our photos, and a one-line Naver review of a hairtail restaurant in Seongsan. So we write the days in order from those, and add what we confirmed on the official Udo Submarine pages. We don’t describe tastes or what the kids said, because we didn’t record them.

Udo Submarine — the key facts (official pages, checked September 2026)

  • 📍 Boards at Seongsan Port Ferry Terminal (submarine ticket office inside). Despite the name, you don’t go to Udo island to ride it — it leaves Seongsan Port and comes back there
  • ⏱ About 1 hour 20 minutes from departure. Arrive 20 minutes before to fill in the boarding form and get tickets
  • 🪪 Original ID for every passenger — for visitors from abroad that means your passport; children need an identity document too (e.g. passport)
  • 💰 Regular sailing: adult 55,000 won on site / 45,000 won online; child (36 months to elementary school) 35,000 / 31,500 won. Early-bird (first sailing only) adult online 35,000 won. Marine park entry is paid separately on site (adult 1,000 / youth 800 / child 500 won)
  • 🅿 Seongsan Port terminal public car park: first 30 min free, then 500 won per 10 min, max 8,000 won a day
  • 🌊 Runs in rain, but sea swells can cancel sailings — check that morning. Full refund if you cancel the day before; no refund for same-day cancellation or no-show

May 2 (Sat), 12:45 pm — our Udo Submarine booking

The center of day one was the Udo Submarine. We booked on Naver on April 29 for Saturday, May 2 at 12:45 pm; the product name included “[Early-bird] special price!!” and the confirmed total was 140,000 won. The confirmation screen didn’t show how many people that covered, so we leave it there.

We have no photos from inside the submarine or underwater. Here is what the official site (jejuseaworld.co.kr) says about the ride itself:

  • The submarine is Yonggung No. 51, up to 54 passengers, with 12 viewing windows on each side.
  • At about 30 m depth you see coral colonies, a feeding show and an “undersea wine” performance.

One thing to note: the official base timetable (9:45 · 10:30 · 11:15 · 13:30 · 14:15 · 15:00) doesn’t include the 12:45 sailing we booked. The site says the schedule changes with sea conditions and minimum numbers, so go by the sailings shown on the booking screen.

May 2 (Sat), 3:48 pm — Udo’s white-stair photo spot

Our next record is at 3:48 pm on Udo island: a white staircase standing on black basalt rocks, leading up into the sky, with “우도 비양 카페” (Udo Biyang Cafe) written on its front. It was overcast that day. The kids climbed to the top and spread their arms, and we took eight photos in two minutes — all of them show their faces, so here we only show the bottom of the stairs.

We have no record of how we crossed from Seongsan to Udo or where else we went on the island.

May 2 (Sat), evening — Seongsan Bada Punggyeong

Dinner on day one was at Seongsan Bada Punggyeong (Seongsan Ilchulbong branch). We have two photos and a one-line Naver review: good value, fast turnover, tasty — and the free whole grilled hairtail was the best part. The keywords we picked were “generous portions”, “good side dishes” and “fresh ingredients”.

Both photos are of banners on the wall. Going by those banners (May 2026), here’s how the place works:

  • Order the braised dish and a whole grilled hairtail comes free: Bada Punggyeong braised fish large-small 39,000 won (small hairtail free) / large-medium 59,000 won (medium) / large-large 79,000 won (large)
  • “Order by how hungry you are, not by the number of people” — the banner says four full people can order small or medium, and two hungry people medium or large
  • Broth simmered for 3+ hours and sauce aged 15+ days, according to the banner; next to the braised fish it says “our cleanup hitter”

  • Bada Punggyeong ttukbaegi (seafood hot pot) 16,000 won — their signature, with assorted seafood, 3-hour broth and a live abalone on top
  • Obunjagi ttukbaegi 19,000 won — only when the haenyeo (women divers) are working
  • Whole Jeju hairtail, grilled small 28,000 / medium 38,000 / large 48,000 won — Jeju silver hairtail grilled whole over direct fire, according to the banner
  • Extra side dishes are self-service

Since our review says “free whole hairtail was the best”, we evidently ordered a braised dish and got the free grilled hairtail, but we have no photo of which size or of the table. The wall clock in the photo read 7:44 pm.

  • Hours listed on the Tabling registration: 10:00–21:00, closed Wednesdays — please check before you go.

May 3 (Sun)

Not a single photo from this day, and no record of where we went, so we leave it blank.

May 4 (Mon), 4:38 pm — leaving Jeju by ferry

The last day’s record is six minutes of photos from 4:38 pm on the deck of a ferry leaving Jeju Port. The deck floor is bright blue, with a photo zone where the letters JEJU stand among a thatched house, a Hallasan-shaped sculpture, a dol hareubang (stone grandfather) and a tangerine sculpture. We took family photos there first (all faces, so not shown). At 4:40 pm one of the kids ran up and down the blue stairs, and from 4:42 pm we stood at the railing watching the sea.

As the ship cleared the breakwater, the sun lit the sea white from behind the clouds. The top photo is at the railing at 4:43 pm; the last one, at 4:44 pm, shows two people walking along the deck, the port and the city of Jeju drifting away behind the rail.

Our ferry route, destination, accommodation and transport weren’t recorded, so we don’t list them.

In one line

Only the first afternoon and the last day’s deck survive from our three days, but two things are immediately useful for the next visitor: the Udo Submarine leaves from and returns to Seongsan Port, and at Seongsan Bada Punggyeong, ordering braised fish gets you a whole grilled hairtail free.
